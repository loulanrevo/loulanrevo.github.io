Went to watch the typhoon.

The promise was made almost out of petty spite after hearing my friend H. talk about storm-chasing in the US.

During an earlier group hike, we had jogged all the way to a flower meadow near the Jimei Bridge, and decided this spot — which always brought *Umi to Hanataba* (Sea and Bouquet) to mind — would be our meeting ground.

In truth, we never really experienced the raw power of a typhoon, just an ordinary, heavy downpour.

My frame of mind had shifted too. Life is a vast and restless sea; all regrets about the past have dissolved into silence. Can emotions with nowhere to rest ever ebb away with the tide?

Walking back to school along the coastline from Fenglin — a commute route I walked for six years, where I knew almost every single building by heart. But after this, I probably won't ever return, nor be able to walk into the campus grounds again.

The spot where we started this time and finished last time was near an observation deck — the one where you had to climb over the guardrail to get in.

Two months later, I returned here. The fierce wind was blowing; standing on the side where the dust had finally settled, looking out into the gray, misty distance.

*A small boat departs from here onward; I entrust the rest of my days to the rivers and seas.*
