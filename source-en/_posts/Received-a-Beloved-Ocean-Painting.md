Prompted by receiving an ocean painting I had fallen in love with, I went to see the sea this week.

I tried taking photos, originally assuming I’d win the day with an early-morning sunrise shoot. But I stayed up too late and slept far too deeply, completely sleeping through the alarm. (Yet by sheer biological clock inertia, I still woke up at 6:30 — proof enough of how terrifying school conditioning is.)

The casual snaps turned out surprisingly decent? Probably just beginner's luck, but feeling a little pleased with myself, I couldn't resist showing them off.

I had originally pictured myself standing in the misty morning air, slipping into some transcendent, enlightened state of oneness with nature, watching the dawn break and letting out poetically melancholic sighs — lines like: *"Keep walking; the shoreline won't hold our footprints for long"*; or *"The ocean sets sail from its homeland, and I awaken from my dream."*

Reality was: severely sleep-deprived. Although I realized in time that I had almost boarded the subway going the wrong way, once I reached the coast, I managed to walk in the exact opposite direction. After clambering across a vast stretch of jagged, pitch-black, rock-hard boulders, I finally found an almost deserted beach ("almost," because there was an elderly gentleman swimming out in the water).

I stepped onto the sand, tasted a drop of seawater, and after barely taking a few steps, came face to face with a massive dead squid.

I beat a lightning-fast retreat back to the road. After begrudgingly finishing the playlist I had carefully curated for my imagined poetic mood, I sighed and went over to the tap to rinse the sand off my feet.

Perhaps this was why the beach was so empty. Just as I thought that, the old swimmer finished his laps, stepped up to the tap beside me, and began casually showering himself with great composure.

He was probably a retiree with time on his hands; I was here simply because I had the day off. The lack of crowds today probably had to do with the pre-holiday makeup workdays for May Day. Thinking about this, a sudden wave of kindred spirit washed over me: *"What night has no moonlight? What place has no bamboo and cypress? It is only that there are few idle wanderers like the two of us."*

I just wonder if he ever noticed that dead squid. It was pretty hard to miss.

My initial thought was that for the first time, I felt a visceral pang of concern over marine ecology.

On second thought, though, I did pass a fishing harbor on the way here. For such a massive squid to turn up near the shore, they must have dropped it off the boat, whether by accident or not. Pay a little more attention next time, will you?
