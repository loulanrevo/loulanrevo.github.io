*Daily Rituals: How Artists Work* (Mason Currey)

## The Impetus

I came across this book while following up on further reading from an article translated by [Jarrett Ye](https://www.zhihu.com/people/JarrettYe): *Cultivating Depth and Stillness in Research*.

![](/img/daily-rituals-expectation.jpg)

The false expectation is believing that progress should always be swift and consistently rewarding, and then spiraling into anxiety and self-doubt when you find yourself out of form.

## What This Book Is About

![](/img/daily-rituals-content.jpg)

Epigraph: *"Who can unravel the essence, the stamp of the artistic temperament! Who can grasp the deep, instinctual fusion of discipline and dissipation on which it rests!"* — Thomas Mann, *Death in Venice*

As mentioned, this book gathers the daily routines, working schedules, and concrete habits of many extraordinary minds:

- **Karl Marx** — Author of *Das Kapital*, foundational theorist of modern communism.
- **Friedrich Nietzsche** — Celebrated philosopher, author of *Thus Spoke Zarathustra*.
- **Ludwig van Beethoven** — The "Titan of Music," renowned for his *Ninth Symphony*.
- **Albert Einstein** — Celebrated not only as a physicist but for his idiosyncratic lifestyle.
- **Ernest Hemingway** — Nobel laureate, author of *The Old Man and the Sea*.
- **Franz Kafka** — Renowned for *The Metamorphosis* and his literature of the absurd.

Reading this serves as reflection on one's own time management. Their monumental achievements lend weight and authority to their methods, and besides, delving into these anecdotes in spare moments is genuinely delightful.

> *"Nearly every weekday morning for a year and a half, I got up at 5:30, brushed my teeth, made a cup of coffee, and sat down to write about how some of the greatest minds of the past four hundred years approached this exact same task — that is, how they made the time each day to do their best work, how they organized their schedules in order to be creative and productive..."*

## Why This Book

As my first semester of university drew to a close, while relishing the greater freedom, I also felt an urgent need for self-management skills. I searched for relevant guides and methodologies, but the internet's information entropy is shockingly low — the same generic advice recycled everywhere, with almost no way of knowing whether anyone actually applied them or how well they worked, while sales pitches and courses were everywhere. While continuing to experiment with techniques, I hoped the historical cases in this book might broaden my perspective.

One must bear in mind that these routines cannot simply be carbon-copied. In particular, one must account for historical differences: practices from the past may have limitations today. In an electronic information society, far more efficient tools and methodologies are available. But that doesn't diminish the value of reading these accounts. People often say: "There are countless methods; find the one that fits you best." I would add: **"Methods cannot be copied blindly, but underlying principles are universal."** I hope that as you read, you too can distill the reasons behind their success and build your own mental models — that is the path that truly works.

Furthermore, I wanted to read and absorb the original English, linking my studies with materials I genuinely enjoy. Learning anecdotes is a refined pleasure in itself; the idea of sharing these stories with others in the future makes me want to collect them. Beyond that, the original prose is brilliant and well worth multiple re-reads.

After reading just a little, I felt an even stronger urge to recommend it, because it described my exact situation:

> *"In reality, most of the people in this book are somewhere in the middle — committed to daily work but never entirely confident of their progress; always wary of the one-off day that undoes the streak."*

Of course, this is simply my personal taste. As the book notes:

> *"John Cheever thought that you couldn't even type a business letter without revealing something of your inner self — isn't that the truth?"*

If you happen to appreciate this style as well, that would be wonderful.

**Here's to wriggling our way through.**

**That is all.**
