Group projects are just wonderful, aren't they?

Two members played dead from start to deadline and haven't sent me a single message to this day.

Two other members, after extensive coaching and back-and-forth from me, just threw raw AI slop right at my face.

One true genius in particular actually rearranged the chronological order of the original text — apparently deciding while copying that Victor Hugo's literary chops weren't quite up to snuff.

I scored a blazing personal record of 0.000001 seconds in the "Don't Lose Your Temper Challenge." Why don't you give it a shot too?

When the project kicked off, in order to maintain team cohesion and prep for the competition, I drafted a full project outline.

Then I spent two whole evening study sessions doing background reading, completely refactoring the outline from scratch.

I wrote comprehensive documents detailing work principles and task allocations: the Virginia incident, excerpts from *Les Misérables*, and all background materials were pre-packaged and shared directly.

If something in the documentation wasn't clear, asking me is totally fine. But after coaching them like middle schoolers on what a summary is, what the difference between fact and argument is, and sinking massive communication costs — what I got in return was logically broken, content-vague AI garbage.

Even though I explicitly stated in both the documents and our chats how to avoid this, some people still just don't get it. The time spent communicating was more than enough for me to finish the entire project alone, without having to reject drafts and revise.

How did things even end up like this?

Joined the group last only to be "warmly welcomed" as team leader; pouring in so much effort with zero return, expected to be the draft horse as a matter of course. Feeling a distinct sense of alienation — how is this not a form of bullying?
